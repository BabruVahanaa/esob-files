Home composting for beginners: what actually works

A beginner-friendly explanation of compost ingredients, airflow, moisture and common problems.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

What composting does

Composting uses microorganisms and other decomposers to break down suitable organic materials into a soil-improving material. A home heap does not need to be complicated, but it benefits from a balance of ingredients, oxygen and moisture.

Mix greens and browns

'Greens' include fresh plant trimmings and many fruit or vegetable scraps; 'browns' include dry leaves, shredded plain cardboard and small woody pieces. The labels describe their role, not necessarily their colour. Mix rather than layering large, compacted masses of one material.

Keep air and moisture in balance

A heap that is very wet and dense may smell unpleasant because oxygen cannot circulate. Add dry, bulky material and turn it gently if practical. A heap that is bone dry decomposes slowly; water it lightly so the contents feel damp rather than soaked.

Know what to leave out

Home composting guidance varies with the system. Avoid adding pet waste, treated wood, persistent weeds with seeds, and material contaminated by chemicals. Meat and dairy can attract pests in ordinary open heaps; follow local council guidance and the instructions for your particular composter.

Use the finished material

Mature compost is typically dark and crumbly, with fewer recognisable scraps and an earthy smell. Screen out larger unfinished pieces and return them to the heap. Use finished compost to improve garden soil rather than assuming it can replace every component of a potting mix.
