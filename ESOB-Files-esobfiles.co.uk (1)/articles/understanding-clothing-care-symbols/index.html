Clothing care symbols explained without the guesswork

Understand washing, drying, ironing and professional-cleaning symbols before your next laundry load.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Start with the care label

Care symbols are a compact set of instructions intended to reduce damage. Read the entire label before washing, especially on mixed-fibre garments, embellished pieces or clothing with special finishes. The instructions are usually based on the most delicate component.

The wash tub

A tub symbol refers to washing. A number generally indicates the maximum wash temperature, while bars beneath the tub signal a gentler process. A hand in the tub indicates hand washing; a crossed-out tub means do not wash. Lower temperatures can be suitable when the fabric and soil level allow.

Triangles, squares and circles

A triangle relates to bleaching. A square concerns drying, including tumble-drying marks, while a circle indicates professional textile care. A circle inside a square relates to tumble drying. Crosses indicate a prohibited treatment; dots commonly communicate temperature intensity.

Ironing and fabric protection

The iron symbol may include dots to indicate heat settings. Avoid direct high heat on prints, coatings or delicate synthetics even when the main fabric seems robust. Turning a garment inside out and using an appropriate pressing cloth can help, but the label remains the first reference.

A practical laundry habit

Sort by colour, soil level and care requirements. When a symbol is unclear, check the retailer's care guidance or a recognised symbol reference rather than guessing. Following the label and avoiding unnecessary heat can help garments retain shape and finish longer.
