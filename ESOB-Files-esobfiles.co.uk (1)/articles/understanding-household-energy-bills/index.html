Understanding your household energy bill in the UK

A plain-English guide to units, standing charges, meter readings and estimated bills.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Separate usage from fixed charges

An energy bill commonly combines a unit rate for the electricity or gas used with a daily standing charge. The standing charge can apply even when consumption is low. Your tariff and payment method affect the amount you pay, so compare like-for-like details.

Read the units

Electricity is typically billed in kilowatt-hours (kWh). Gas meters measure volume, which suppliers convert into kWh using a calculation shown on bills. Check whether the reading is actual, customer-submitted or estimated, as estimates can make a bill diverge from real use.

Check the billing period

A high total may cover more days than expected or include an earlier balance. Look at the opening and closing readings, dates, unit rates, standing charges, VAT and payments already made. Compare consumption over similar periods rather than looking only at the final amount.

Understand price information

A price cap is not a cap on an individual household's total bill; actual cost still depends on usage and tariff conditions. Rules and rates can change, so use current supplier documents and Ofgem guidance when checking an offer or a disputed charge.

What to do if something looks wrong

Record your meter reading, compare it with the bill and contact your supplier with the dates and figures. Keep copies of correspondence. If the issue is not resolved, follow the supplier's complaints process and the applicable independent escalation route.
