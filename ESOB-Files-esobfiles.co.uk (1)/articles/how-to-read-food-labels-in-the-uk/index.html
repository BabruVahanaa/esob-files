How to read food labels in the UK

A practical guide to ingredients, nutrition tables, serving sizes and front-of-pack claims on UK groceries.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Start with the whole package

Food packaging is designed to be scanned quickly, but the front is not a substitute for the detailed label. Begin with the product name, net quantity and ingredients list, then use the nutrition panel to compare alternatives. The most useful comparison is often between similar products rather than between unrelated foods.

Ingredients and allergens

Ingredients are generally listed in descending order by weight at the point of manufacture. Look for highlighted allergens and any separate precautionary allergen statement. A 'may contain' warning is different from a declared ingredient; people with allergies should follow individual medical advice and the manufacturer's current packaging.

Per 100 g versus per portion

UK nutrition tables commonly give values per 100 g or 100 ml; this provides a consistent basis for comparing brands. Portion figures can be useful for planning, but portions vary between products and may not match what you actually eat. Compare energy, saturated fat, sugars, salt and fibre in context.

Claims need context

Words such as 'natural', 'protein-rich' or 'no added sugar' do not tell the whole nutritional story. A product without added sugar may still contain naturally occurring sugars. Check the actual quantities and serving size before drawing conclusions.

A sensible shopping routine

Choose two comparable products, read the ingredients and per-100 g figures, then consider taste, price and how often you will use them. Keep the comparison practical: the best label is one that helps you make an informed choice rather than promises a perfect diet.
