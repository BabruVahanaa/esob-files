How to start a small balcony herb garden

Grow a useful selection of herbs in containers by matching light, watering and pot size.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Observe the space

Before buying plants, notice how many hours of direct light your balcony receives and whether it is windy or sheltered. Many Mediterranean herbs prefer bright conditions and freely draining soil, while some leafy herbs tolerate less direct sun.

Choose containers carefully

Use pots with drainage holes and a suitable peat-free potting mix where possible. Containers dry out faster than garden beds, particularly in warm or windy weather. Check balcony weight limits and secure pots so they cannot fall.

Start with a manageable selection

Parsley, chives and mint are approachable options, but their needs differ. Mint can spread aggressively and is often best grown in its own container. Rosemary and thyme generally dislike sitting in persistently wet compost. Buy healthy plants rather than starting with too many varieties.

Water by need, not the calendar

Check moisture below the surface before watering. Some plants need frequent attention in summer, while others prefer to dry slightly between waterings. Water thoroughly when needed and let excess drain away; standing water can damage roots.

Harvest to encourage growth

Take small amounts regularly, avoiding stripping a young plant. Remove damaged growth and watch for pests. Seasonal light and temperature changes affect productivity, so treat a balcony herb garden as a small experiment rather than expecting year-round harvests.
