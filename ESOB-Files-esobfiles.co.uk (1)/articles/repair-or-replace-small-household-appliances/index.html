Repair or replace? A guide to small household appliances

How to assess cost, safety, parts availability and environmental impact before replacing an appliance.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Define the fault first

A broken appliance is not always beyond repair. Check the manufacturer's troubleshooting guide for basic issues such as blocked filters, worn seals or an incorrect setting. Unplug equipment before inspection, and never bypass safety devices or open electrical components without appropriate training.

Compare the full cost

The decision is more than the price of a new unit. Consider a professional diagnostic fee, replacement parts, labour, warranty coverage and expected remaining life. A repair that is inexpensive relative to a reliable replacement may be worthwhile, especially for a product you already like.

Look for parts and support

Search the model number on the manufacturer's support pages. Availability of genuine parts, published repair instructions and an authorised service network can make a substantial difference. Check whether a repair will preserve any warranty and whether the work requires a qualified technician.

Think beyond the purchase price

Manufacturing and shipping replacements use resources, while older equipment can sometimes consume more electricity. The balance depends on the appliance, its use and the efficiency difference. Avoid broad claims that repairing is always greener or replacing is always cheaper.

Make a safe decision

If a device overheats, smells of burning, has damaged wiring or trips protection repeatedly, stop using it and seek qualified advice. For ordinary faults, request a written estimate and compare it with a suitable replacement's price, warranty and expected lifespan.
