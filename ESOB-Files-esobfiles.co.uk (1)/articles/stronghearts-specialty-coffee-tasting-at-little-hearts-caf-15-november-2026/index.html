Saltash specialty coffee tasting brings together guests from Saltash and Looe

A small-group tasting at Little Hearts Café on 15 November 2026 brought coffee enthusiasts from Saltash and Looe together to explore the Stronghearts range.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 10 November 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Event report · Sunday 15 November 2026. This article updates our earlier event preview with information and photographs supplied after the gathering.

A local gathering around specialty coffee

On Sunday 15 November 2026, Little Hearts Café in Saltash hosted a specialty coffee tasting organised by Stronghearts. Guests travelled from Saltash and Looe for the small-group event, bringing together people with a shared curiosity about the flavours, origins and differences between coffees. The gathering followed an advance announcement that all ten available places had been booked.

Inside Little Hearts Café

The photographs supplied to ESOB Files show the tasting taking shape inside the café: a welcome sign on a wooden chalkboard, coffee bags arranged along a window-side table, and individual glass bowls containing beans. The setting was informal and intimate, with the display positioned against the café's familiar yellow walls and dark timber windows. Rather than a trade-show setting, this was a local space where participants could sit together and compare what was in front of them.

Four coffees on display

The event photographs show four Stronghearts coffee bags labelled Colombiana, Como Italia, Monsoon Ranga and Massa Forte. Their labels provide a visible record of the selection displayed on the tasting table. Those names should not be mistaken for independently verified tasting notes: the photographs do not establish precise brewing recipes, scores or the flavour preferences of individual guests.

From the display table to the tasting cups

Later photographs show participants seated at tables with several white cups, glasses of water, coffee bags, pens and printed tasting sheets. Some guests are pictured smelling or sipping from cups; others appear to be writing or discussing their impressions. This is the distinctive appeal of a guided tasting: several coffees can be considered side by side, encouraging people to notice differences rather than judging a single cup in isolation.

Why tasting together matters

Coffee vocabulary can seem technical, but a shared tasting makes it more approachable. Aroma, body, sweetness, acidity and aftertaste are useful starting points for discussion. A person's response can vary with preparation, temperature and personal preference; there is no need for everyone around the table to reach the same conclusion. The images document active participation, not a formal competition or a scored judging panel.

A connection between Saltash and Looe

With guests from Saltash and Looe, the event had a wider local reach than one neighbourhood. Small gatherings like this create opportunities for people from nearby Cornish communities to meet in person and explore a subject together. The supplied photographs capture that community dimension through shared tables, note-taking and conversation.

The organiser and venue

Stronghearts organised the specialty coffee tasting, while Little Hearts Café provided the venue in Saltash. The coffee packaging and event poster prominently feature the Stronghearts name. Readers interested in the organiser and its coffee range can visit stronghearts.co.uk. For café updates, see Little Hearts Café on Instagram or its Facebook page.

What the photographs confirm — and what they do not

This report is based on event information supplied to ESOB Files and the accompanying photographs, rather than an independent on-site review. The images show the coffee display, tasting materials and guests taking part. They do not independently verify individual guest identities, specific tasting scores, quotations, a full programme or any award outcomes. No attendee quotations or invented tasting verdicts are presented here.

A memorable local format

The strongest story from the 15 November gathering is straightforward: a small, fully booked specialty coffee event brought people from Saltash and Looe together at Little Hearts Café. The images preserve both the careful setup and the human side of the occasion — cups on the table, pens at the ready and guests giving each sample their attention.
