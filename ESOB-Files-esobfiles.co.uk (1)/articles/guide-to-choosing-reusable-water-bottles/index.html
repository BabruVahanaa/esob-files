Choosing a reusable water bottle: materials and practical trade-offs

Compare stainless steel, glass and plastic bottles by cleaning, durability, weight and everyday use.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Begin with how you use it

A bottle for commuting has different requirements from one kept on a desk. Consider capacity, leak resistance, weight, ease of opening and whether it fits your bag or cup holder. A product you regularly carry is more useful than one with an impressive feature list.

Stainless steel

Steel bottles are generally durable, and double-walled versions can help keep drinks at a more stable temperature. They can be heavier and more expensive than simple plastic bottles. Inspect seals and lids, since these parts often determine whether a bottle leaks.

Glass and plastic

Glass is easy to inspect and can resist retaining flavours, but it is heavier and breakable. Reusable plastic is often light and affordable, though it may scratch or retain odours over time. Check the manufacturer's food-contact and temperature guidance rather than assuming every material is suitable for hot liquids.

Cleaning matters most

Choose a bottle with a wide enough opening and accessible lid components. Wash and dry it as the maker instructs, paying attention to gaskets and drinking spouts. Replace damaged seals, and avoid putting parts in a dishwasher unless they are marked as dishwasher-safe.

Buy for longevity

Replacement lids and seals, a reliable warranty and an uncomplicated design can matter more than colour or branding. Compare the bottle's likely daily use with its cost; the best choice is one you can clean easily and keep using.
