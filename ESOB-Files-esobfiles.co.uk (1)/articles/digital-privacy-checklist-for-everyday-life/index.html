An everyday digital privacy checklist

Ten-minute habits for stronger passwords, safer accounts, app permissions and device updates.

By ESOB Files editorial desk · Published 17 November 2026

Secure your most important accounts

Start with email, banking and the accounts that can reset other passwords. Use a unique, strong password for each service and consider a reputable password manager. Reusing one password across sites makes a single breach more damaging.

Turn on multi-factor authentication

Enable multi-factor authentication where offered, especially for email and financial accounts. Authenticator apps or security keys can offer stronger protection than relying only on text messages. Save recovery codes somewhere secure and separate from the device you use every day.

Review permissions

Check which apps can access your location, contacts, microphone, camera and photos. Remove permissions that are not needed for the app's purpose. Review connected third-party services and revoke access you no longer recognise or use.

Keep software current

Install security updates for your phone, computer, browser and router. Turn on automatic updates where appropriate, and verify that devices still receive manufacturer support. Unsupported software may remain exposed to vulnerabilities that are no longer fixed.

Watch for pressure tactics

Phishing messages often create urgency or ask you to follow a link to 'verify' an account. Open the service through its official app or a bookmarked address instead. A privacy routine works best when repeated periodically, not treated as a one-off clean-up.
